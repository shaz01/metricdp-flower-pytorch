\noindent
\code{metric-privacy} distance metric: for every pair of sampled client models, compute the
Euclidean (L2) distance between each corresponding layer, then average across layers to get one
scalar per pair; $d$ is the \emph{maximum} of these mean layer-wise distances over all client
pairs in the round. Noise scale that round is \code{noise\_multiplier}$/d$ (vs.\ \code{global-dp}'s
flat \code{noise\_multiplier}, independent of client-model spread).

\section*{Metric-Privacy Distance Metric ($d$), Training Values}

\begin{table}[h!]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{llrrrr}
\toprule
Dataset & Partition & $d$ (round 1) & $d$ (mid-training) & $d$ (final round) & noise\_stdv (final) \\
\midrule
CIFAR-100 & homogeneous & 1.709 & 1.244 & 1.243 & 0.000732 \\
CIFAR-100 & non-iid & 1.862 & 1.372 & 1.349 & 0.000674 \\
EuroSAT & homogeneous & 0.517 & 0.377 & 0.410 & 0.009436 \\
EuroSAT & non-iid & 0.631 & 0.401 & 0.442 & 0.008748 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption*{Mid-training = round 126/250 (CIFAR-100), round 51/100 (EuroSAT). \code{noise\_stdv}
is the actual Gaussian noise standard deviation applied that round
(\code{noise\_multiplier}$/d$, scaled by clipping norm). No round on any combo ever hit a
non-finite/invalid distance or an aggregation collapse.}
\end{table}

\noindent
$d$ drops sharply in the first part of training (client models start far apart, then converge
toward each other) and levels off or drifts back up slightly rather than continuing to shrink,
which is why \code{noise\_stdv} rises early and then roughly stabilizes rather than blowing up;
EuroSAT's $d$ values are noticeably smaller than CIFAR-100's, consistent with EuroSAT's much
smaller model (289K vs.\ 4.6M parameters).
