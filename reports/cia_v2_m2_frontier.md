## 1. Question

M1 showed that v1's flat noise response came from shared noise. M2 measures what an attacker who sees one world
actually achieves against global DP, under the v2 measurement standard, and how that falls with noise.

The hypothesis: there is a noise level `r_0.6` at which the calibrated attacker's AUC is 0.6 or below while mean
accuracy stays at 0.70 or above, a useful empirical regime. The alternative: leakage stays above 0.6 until accuracy
collapses.

## 2. Protocol

**Federation.** Randomized inclusion (`results/cia_v2/randomized_inclusion/runner.py`, M0's final design):

- EuroSAT, label-Dirichlet alpha 0.3, the seed-42 layout, 48 slots, 50 rounds, FedAvg with equal weights, WP2
  hyperparameters.
- 32 candidates (the top decile 23, 7, 40, 18, 33, then 27 by size quantile); 16 IN per replicate, so 32 active
  clients.
- Shadow samples and label-matched surrogates of every candidate scored at rounds 0, 5, ..., 50.

**Replicates.** `R = 32` per configuration, `design_seed = 2`. Replicate `j` has the same inclusion and the same
`(client_seed, noise_seed)` in every configuration, which pairs configurations; within a configuration no two runs
share private randomness.

**Configurations.** Vanilla, and global DP with noise std `r C` (noise multiplier `32 r`) at multiples of the
frontier ratio 0.001546: 1x, 4x and 16x, plus fill-in levels by the rule below.

**Order.**

1. Stage 1: replicates 0-15 of vanilla, 1x, 4x and 16x.
2. **Fill-in rule** (accuracy only, fixed in advance): add 2x if stage-1 mean accuracy at 1x exceeds that at 4x by more
   than 0.05; add 8x on the same rule for 4x against 16x. A fill-in level runs both stages.
3. Stage 2: replicates 16-31 of every configuration, always.
4. No leakage statistic before stage 2 of every configuration is complete.

**Attacker and intervals.** The calibrated loss attacker (M0) on the 11-checkpoint trajectory, for all candidates and
the top decile. Gate interval: pair jackknife at 97.5%; the two-way bootstrap (2,000 resamples) alongside. Accuracy
with a 95% bootstrap over replicates. Formal `mu_total = sqrt(50) / (32 r)`.

**Gate G-M2 as applied** (M0's escalation resolution, item 4). A noise level is a useful regime when:

1. the all-candidate AUC's upper gate-interval end is 0.6 or below;
2. the top-decile point estimate is 0.6 or below;
3. the lower interval end of mean accuracy is 0.70 or above.

**Power** (M0's table, `R = 32`): detectable difference at 80% power 0.087 (all candidates) and 0.196 (top decile);
a single configuration's gate half-width is about 0.05.

## 3. Stage 1 and the fill-in decision

Stage 1 (64 runs, 12-13 minutes each) gave mean final accuracies of 0.803 (vanilla), 0.788 (1x), 0.653 (4x) and 0.182
(16x). 1x minus 4x is 0.136 and 4x minus 16x is 0.470, both above 0.05, so 2x and 8x were added. No leakage statistic
was computed at this point.

`analysis.json` also holds TPR at 1% FPR, the uncalibrated threshold attacker and the surrogate-attacker AUC (B5's
input) for both candidate sets.

## 5. Reading

- **Leakage barely moves until the model breaks.** From vanilla to 2x the AUC goes from 0.723 to 0.702 while accuracy
  falls by 0.06. It reaches 0.593 only at 16x.
- **With independent randomness, leakage does fall with noise, but slowly.** The decline from vanilla to 16x is 0.13
  AUC. Each step from 1x to 8x is within the 0.087 detectable difference, so adjacent levels cannot be told apart.
- **The largest clients stay exposed.** The top-decile AUC is 0.72-0.75 from vanilla to 8x, and 0.603 only at 16x.
- **The formal guarantee is vacuous wherever accuracy is usable:** `mu_total` 71-143 at the levels with accuracy of
  0.70 or above, and 8.9 only at 16x.
- **Against M1.** M1's decoupled paired score was at chance at 4x (0.516), while M2's calibrated attacker gets 0.666 at
  the same noise. The difference is the attacker: v1's statistic compares one IN and one OUT trajectory per target,
  while the calibrated attacker pools 32 replicates and calibrates each candidate's difficulty.

## 7. Anomalies and corrections

- **GPU change between stages.** Stage 1 ran on GPU 1 (shared by owner approval). Before stage 2 another user's
  active job appeared on GPU 1, so stage 2 ran on GPU 0, which was idle and unclaimed. The runs do not depend on the
  device.
- **Merge between stages.** The private-median mode for A1 was merged into this branch between the stages. The 64
  stage-1 manifests were byte-identical under the merged code.
- **Workers sharing a configuration** sometimes logged "complete 15/16" after three passes while the other worker
  held the last run; it completed in every case (16/16).

## 8. Caveats

- One dataset, one layout seed, one heterogeneity level, one attacker family (loss trajectories).
- The gate interval conditions on the 32-candidate panel; the two-way bootstrap, which generalises over clients, gives
  the same verdict.
- The realized-noise-energy correlation across configurations (STATE.md, "shared-noise check") was not computed by
  `analyze.py`. The pairing holds by construction (equal seeds per replicate), and M2 compares no configurations
  statistically.
- **The calibrated attacker is not robust to diverged trajectories** (found in A2): one diverged run in the references
  can reverse its direction. That can only make measured leakage lower than an attacker could achieve, so it does not
  threaten "no useful regime"; at 8x-16x, where runs start to diverge, the AUCs may understate leakage.
