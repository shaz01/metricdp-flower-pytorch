\noindent
This document reports the two constant-compute client-count-scaling sweeps that hold total
training compute roughly constant across $n \in \{4, 8, 48\}$ federated clients
(Alzheimer MRI classification, $\text{noise\_multiplier}=0.05$, seed 42, clipping norm 5.0):
\textbf{v1 (round-scaled)} scales the number of communication rounds with $n$
($\text{rounds}(n) = \text{round}(20 \cdot n / 4)$) while holding local epochs fixed at 5;
\textbf{v2 (epoch-scaled)} instead fixes rounds at 20 and scales local epochs with $n$
($\text{local\_epochs}(n) = \text{round}(5 \cdot n / 4)$). Source data:
\code{results/scale\_controlled/} (v1) and \code{results/scale\_controlled\_epochs/} (v2); sweep
code: \code{experiments/client\_scaling/sweep\_scale\_controlled.py} and
\code{sweep\_scale\_controlled\_epochs.py}; full narrative writeup:
\code{reports/constant\_compute\_scaling.md}. Accuracy/loss/F1/precision/AUC are the
training-time recorded values from the last completed round; ``Invalid''/``Collapsed'' are
per-round counts of \code{metric-dp-distance-invalid}/\code{metric-dp-aggregation-collapsed}
flags (metric-privacy only); distance min/mean/max pools every round's finite
\code{metric-dp-distance-mean} value.

% =====================================================================
\section*{Motivation and Expected vs.\ Observed Outcome}
% =====================================================================

\noindent \textbf{Why.} \code{results/48client\_scaling} (fixed 20-round budget for every client
count) found metric-privacy's accuracy advantage over global-DP shrinking or reversing as $n$ grows
from 8 to 48. That comparison has a built-in confound: under a \emph{fixed} round budget, each
client's per-round data shard shrinks roughly $\propto 1/n$, so a 48-client run gives each client
far less data per round than an 8-client run. The shrinking advantage could therefore be either
(a) a genuine failure of the metric-privacy mechanism at scale, or (b) an artifact of clients being
under-trained at high $n$ because of the fixed round budget --- not the mechanism's fault at all.
v1 and v2 hold total training compute roughly constant across $n$ instead of the round budget, to
disentangle these two explanations.

\noindent \textbf{Expected output.} If (b) --- the round-budget confound --- were the whole story,
the metric-privacy-vs-global-DP advantage should stay roughly flat across $n \in \{4,8,48\}$ under
constant compute, instead of shrinking, since every client count now receives a comparable amount
of total local training.

\noindent \textbf{What differs from that expectation.} The advantage does \emph{not} stay flat: it
still shrinks steadily from $n{=}4$ to $n{=}48$ in both v1 and v2, landing close to zero (and
turning slightly negative for \code{homogeneous}/\code{fedavg} and \code{fedyogi}) rather than
holding constant. So the round-budget confound is not the whole story --- it explains part of the
effect (shrinkage under constant compute is far milder than the sharp reversal seen under the
fixed-round-budget baseline), but a real, smaller scale-related narrowing of the advantage remains
even after removing the confound. A second surprise: v1 and v2 do not converge to the same absolute
accuracy at $n{=}48$ (v1 reaches 91--98\%, v2 plateaus at 77--79\%), showing that scaling local
epochs is not a drop-in substitute for scaling rounds --- aggregation frequency itself matters
independently of total local compute.

\noindent
Accuracy climbs sharply with $n$ under both privacy modes in v1 (e.g.\ homogeneous/global-dp/fedavg:
$49.53\% \to 79.69\% \to 97.50\%$; homogeneous/metric-privacy/fedavg: $63.44\% \to 90.47\% \to
95.63\%$), and every metric-privacy combination completes its full round budget with zero
invalid-distance or collapsed-aggregation rounds, at any $n$. The metric-privacy advantage over
global-dp is largest at $n{=}4$ (up to $+33.91\pp$, non-IID/fedavg) and narrows steadily as $n$
grows, landing close to parity by $n{=}48$ and even turning slightly negative for
homogeneous/fedavg ($95.63\%$ vs.\ $97.50\%$) and homogeneous/fedyogi ($72.19\%$ vs.\ $74.69\%$ at
$n{=}8$) --- consistent with round-scaling giving the model far more total local steps at $n{=}48$
(240 rounds) than at $n{=}4$ (20 rounds), which increasingly favors whichever mechanism tolerates
more aggregation events.

\noindent
With rounds held fixed at 20 and local epochs scaled instead ($5 \to 10 \to 60$), v2 again shows
zero invalid-distance or collapsed-aggregation rounds across every metric-privacy combination, and
the same narrowing-advantage pattern as $n$ grows. The absolute numbers diverge from v1 at
$n{=}48$, however: v2 plateaus around $77$--$79\%$ for both privacy modes on both partitions
(vs.\ v1's $91$--$98\%$), since aggregation frequency itself never increases beyond 20 rounds ---
scaling local epochs alone cannot substitute for more aggregation events at high $n$. This design is
also far cheaper to run: total wall-clock across all 20 combinations is $\approx 1.83$h for v2
versus $\approx 4.34$h for v1, almost entirely because v1's $n{=}48$ combinations run 240 rounds
while v2's stay at 20.
