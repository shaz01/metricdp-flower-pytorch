# CIA roadmap v2, M1: shared-noise confirmation

Package M1 of `research/cia_roadmap_v2/ROADMAP.md` (Agent A, branch `feature/cia-v2-measurement`, 2026-10-17). The
owner closed the package on 2026-10-20.

All numbers below come from committed artifacts in `results/cia_v2/measurement/m1/`: `STATE.md` (design and gate,
fixed before any run), `FINDINGS.md`, `analysis.json` and the 12 trajectories in `results/`. `analysis.json` is
regenerated by `python -m results.cia_v2.measurement.m1.analyze --out results/cia_v2/measurement/m1/analysis.json`.

## 1. Question

In v1's consensus-KL step 3, global DP at 1, 2 and 4 times the frontier noise gave paired clean-view scores of 0.788,
0.808 and 0.792: leakage that did not respond to noise. In that design the IN and OUT trajectories drew identical
server noise and shared the client training seed. Their difference then contained only the target's own signal, so
it could not shrink as the noise grew.

M1 asks whether that flat response is an artifact. It repeats v1's experiment with one change, independent private
randomness per world, and keeps v1's statistic on purpose so that only the coupling changes.

## 2. Protocol

**Setup.** v1's step-3 global-DP arm (`results/cia_frontier/consensus_kl/runner_dp.py`, mechanism `gdp`):

- EuroSAT, alpha 0.3, the seed-42 layout, 48 clients, 50 rounds, equal weights, WP2 hyperparameters.
- Global DP with noise std `r C`, fixed across removal, at `r = 0.001546` (1x) and `0.006184` (4x).

**Worlds.** Removal adjacency with targets 23, 7, 1, 0 and 3. Per noise level, one IN trajectory and five OUT
trajectories: 12 trajectories.

**The one change.** Each of the 6 worlds gets its own `client_seed` and `noise_seed` from `SeedSequence([1, world])`.
No two worlds share private randomness. The same world at 1x and 4x reuses its seeds (allowed across
configurations). Public randomness is shared as in v1: initial model, layout, server test split, shadow samples.

**Statistic** (v1's, deliberately). Per target, the fraction of rounds 1-50 at which the OUT trajectory's clean shadow
loss exceeds the IN trajectory's, pooled over targets with v1's `cell_statistics`. Interval: v1's 2,000-resample
cluster bootstrap over the five targets. It is reported as an effect size, not attack success (measurement standard,
Section 2 item 6).

**Gate G-M1** (ROADMAP.md, on the 4x point estimate):

| 4x decoupled paired score | Verdict | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| 0.70 or below | Confirmed (prediction: 0.62 or below) | v1 DP leakage numbers flagged; M2 proceeds |
| Between 0.70 and 0.75 | Partial | M2 proceeds; the 1x result is reported alongside |
| 0.75 or above | Not material | The flat noise response is real; escalate, since B4 then becomes the main line |

**Code checks before any run.** The runner differs from v1's only in the private seeds and the run names (7 tests).
On v1's coupled trajectories the analysis reproduces v1's 0.788 / 0.808 / 0.792 exactly, with IN-vs-OUT noise-energy
correlations of 0.925-0.997 (a regression test in `tests/test_m1_analyze.py`).

## 3. Result: G-M1 confirmed

| Noise (`r`) | Paired score, decoupled | 95% interval | Coupled (v1) | Oracle-midpoint AUC | Top decile (23, 7) | Mean final accuracy (range) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1x (0.001546) | 0.788 | 0.632-0.928 | 0.788 | 0.822 | 0.90 | 0.797 (0.784-0.818) |
| 4x (0.006184) | **0.516** | 0.468-0.548 | 0.792 | 0.506 | 0.55 | 0.668 (0.645-0.688) |

Per target (fraction of rounds at which OUT's clean shadow loss exceeds IN's):

| Target | 0 | 1 | 3 | 7 | 23 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1x | 0.62 | 0.54 | 0.98 | 0.92 | 0.88 |
| 4x | 0.52 | 0.42 | 0.54 | 0.54 | 0.56 |

**Decoupling check.** The realized-noise-energy correlation between the IN trajectory and each OUT trajectory, over 50
rounds, is -0.241 to 0.078 at 1x and -0.180 to 0.038 at 4x. The largest magnitudes, from target 3, are about 1.7
standard errors of a zero correlation (1/sqrt(50) = 0.14). v1's coupled trajectories gave 0.925-0.997. The runs drew
independent noise, as designed.

## 4. Reading

- **v1's flat noise response was an artifact of shared noise.** At 4x, independent noise removes the effect: both the
  paired score (0.516) and the oracle-midpoint AUC (0.506) are at chance. v1's 0.792 at 4x came from IN and OUT
  drawing the same noise.
- **At 1x the effect survives decoupling.** The paired score stays at 0.788 and the top decile at 0.90, so leakage
  at the frontier noise is real. Between 1x and 4x it falls to chance in this statistic, while mean accuracy drops by
  0.13.
- **Consequence:** M2 proceeds, and from then on every v2 document labels v1's DP leakage numbers "paired, shared
  noise".

## 5. Anomalies and corrections

- `FINDINGS.md` first cited a wrong test file (`test_analyze.py` instead of `test_m1_analyze.py`) and a loose cost
  wording; both were corrected before the result commit.

## 6. Caveats

- One seed and one IN trajectory per noise level, so every target's comparison shares that IN trajectory.
- The interval treats the five targets as clusters and is not calibrated.
- The paired score is v1's statistic, kept so that only the coupling changes. It is not the v2 attacker. M2 later
  measured leakage with the calibrated attacker under randomized inclusion, and found it much higher at 4x (all
  candidates AUC 0.666): a decoupled paired score at chance does not mean that no attacker succeeds.
