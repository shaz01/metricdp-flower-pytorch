\begin{abstract}
\noindent
This report presents the results of redoing Phase 1's constant-compute client-count scaling
diagnosis on CUDA hardware, after fixing a genuine floating-point non-determinism source in
aggregation (client replies were being summed in network-arrival order rather than a deterministic
order) and moving off this project's original MPS backend, whose own documented non-determinism had
made every prior result on this question untrustworthy. Two experimental designs (\code{v1},
round-scaled; \code{v2}, epoch-scaled) were each run to completion across the \code{fedavg} matrix
($n \in \{4,8,48\}$) plus \code{fedyogi} at $n \in \{4,8\}$: \textbf{40 of 40 combinations
completed, 0 run failures, 0 invalid-distance or collapsed-aggregation rounds anywhere.}
Determinism was confirmed directly, not just inferred. The central question this diagnosis exists
to answer --- whether the metric-privacy-vs-global-dp accuracy advantage's collapse at scale is a
genuine mechanism failure or a measurement artifact --- now has a trustworthy answer for
\code{fedavg}: the advantage does shrink from $n{=}4$ to $n{=}48$, but converges toward parity, not
the large reversal the earlier MPS-era attempt reported (and which never survived its own
noise-floor check). \code{fedyogi} at $n{=}48$ is the immediate next step, not yet run.
\end{abstract}

\section{Background and What Changed}
% =====================================================================

\code{results/48client\_scaling} (fixed 20-round budget) found the metric-privacy-vs-global-dp
accuracy advantage shrinking or reversing at 48 clients compared to 8. This raised the question this
diagnosis was built to answer: is that a genuine mechanism failure at scale, or an artifact of
holding the round budget fixed while each client's per-round data shrinks roughly as $1/n$? An
earlier attempt at this diagnosis ran on this project's original Mac (MPS backend) and was
superseded --- \code{reports/archive/constant\_compute\_scaling\_mps\_v1v2.md} documents that its
own \emph{identical}, same-seed configuration produced accuracy deltas of over $50\pp$ across two
runs, proving the measurement platform itself had a noise floor comparable to the effect sizes in
question. No result from that attempt should be cited as established.

Two things changed for this redo:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
    \item \textbf{A genuine non-determinism source was found and fixed.} Flower's own weighted
    aggregation summed client replies in network-arrival order rather than a deterministic order;
    floating-point addition is not associative, so run-to-run arrival jitter produced numeric drift
    that compounded over rounds. Fixed via \code{DeterministicReplyOrderMixin}
    (\code{metricdp\_pytorch/strategy\_factory.py}) and a matching fix in \code{metricdp\_pytorch/metrics.py},
    sorting by the already-present per-reply \code{client-id} before aggregating.
    \item \textbf{Training moved onto CUDA hardware}, off the MPS backend entirely.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Experimental designs}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
    \item \textbf{v1 (round-scaled)}: $\text{rounds}(n) = \text{round}(20 \cdot n / 4)$,
    \code{local-epochs}/\code{batch-size} held fixed (5, 32). Total local gradient steps per client
    stay roughly constant across $n$, at the cost of scaling aggregation frequency 12$\times$ from
    $n{=}4$ to $n{=}48$.
    \item \textbf{v2 (epoch-scaled)}: $\text{rounds}(n) = 20$ (fixed),
    $\text{local\_epochs}(n) = \text{round}(5 \cdot n / 4)$ instead --- the corrected design,
    targeting aggregation-frequency parity directly.
\end{itemize}
Matrix: \code{global-dp}/\code{metric-privacy} $\times$ \code{homogeneous}/\code{non-iid} $\times$
$n \in \{4,8,48\}$, $\text{noise\_multiplier}=0.05$, seed 42, clipping norm 5.0 --- plus
\code{fedyogi} at $n \in \{4,8\}$ (not yet run at $n{=}48$).

\noindent The advantage shrinks steadily from $n{=}4$ to $n{=}48$ in both designs, landing close to
zero rather than reversing sharply. Compare the archived MPS report's v1/homogeneous trajectory:
$+21.41\pp \to +5.62\pp \to \mathbf{-18.91\pp}$ --- a reversal that later failed its own direct
noise-floor check and was never established as real. This redo's $n{=}48$ deltas ($-2.50$ to
$+0.62\pp$) are an order of magnitude smaller and agree in direction between v1 and v2 (near-zero,
not a reversal). This is the first time this question has been asked on a platform confirmed not to
have a noise floor comparable to the effect size.

\subsection{Determinism confirmed directly}

v1 and v2's $n{=}4$ combinations share identical hyperparameters by construction. Every pair ---
\code{fedavg} and \code{fedyogi}, both partitions --- produced \textbf{bit-identical} accuracy and
loss from two independently-launched subprocesses, verified directly against the raw result JSONs.
The archived MPS report's equivalent check found the opposite: MPS's v1/v2 $n{=}4$ results
\emph{disagreed} with each other on an identical configuration, diverging already at round 0 ---
before any client aggregation ---proving MPS's own model-initialization non-determinism, distinct
from and larger than the aggregation-order bug this redo fixes.

\subsection{The aggregation-order fix covers every aggregation method}

\code{DeterministicReplyOrderMixin} wraps \code{FedAvg}, \code{FedYogi}, \code{FedAvgM},
\code{FedMedian}, and \code{FedProx} uniformly in
\code{strategy\_factory.make\_base\_strategy} --- this is why \code{global-dp}'s own numbers
improved as dramatically as metric-privacy's (e.g.\ v1 homogeneous/global-dp/$n{=}48$:
$33.44\% \to 97.50\%$). The archived MPS \code{fedavg}/\code{global-dp}/$n{=}48$ trajectory
plateaued at 15--42\% accuracy for all 240 rounds without ever really learning, despite no loss
spikes --- a distinct failure mode from metric-privacy's collapsed-round pathology, traceable to
the same root cause.

\subsection{fedyogi at $n{=}8$: one combination worth watching}

v1's \code{homogeneous}/\code{fedyogi} delta goes \emph{negative} already at $n{=}8$
($+11.56\pp \to \mathbf{-2.50\pp}$) --- the only $n \leq 8$ combination across both designs and
both aggregation methods where metric-privacy underperforms global-dp. v2's
\code{homogeneous}/\code{fedyogi} delta stays positive at $n{=}8$ ($+11.56\pp \to +6.57\pp$). Both
designs agree \code{non-iid}/\code{fedyogi} shrinks but stays positive at $n{=}8$ (v1:
$+36.88\pp \to +7.35\pp$; v2: $+36.88\pp \to +6.56\pp$). This is the specific combination
$n{=}48$ \code{fedyogi} data (\S\ref{sec:next}) will resolve.

\section{Known Gaps and Caveats}
% =====================================================================

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
    \item \textbf{\code{fedyogi} at $n{=}48$ has not been run yet.} \S\ref{sec:findings}'s delta
    table and the fedyogi observation above are therefore only established for \code{fedavg} at
    $n{=}48$.
    \item \textbf{Single seed (42) for every run.} The determinism fix guarantees a given seed
    reproduces exactly, not that a different seed would land the same deltas.
    \item \textbf{Reduced matrix.} \code{vanilla}, \code{fedavgm}, \code{fedprox},
    \code{fedmedian}, and $n{=}16$ remain out of scope.
    \item \textbf{The fixed-20-round $n{=}48$ baseline was not rerun on CUDA} --- still MPS-era
    data; the round-fragmentation explanation for why constant-compute accuracy under-performs it
    is unchanged from the archived report.
    \item \textbf{Not yet merged to \code{master}.} Code and results live on
    \code{feature/scale-controlled-redo}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Planned Next Steps}
\label{sec:next}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Immediate}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
    \item Run \code{fedyogi} at $n{=}48$ on both designs, completing the matrix this redo set out
    to run and resolving whether \S\ref{sec:findings}'s \code{homogeneous}/\code{fedyogi} $n{=}8$
    reversal continues or corrects at scale.
    \item Revisit whether the central question is fully answered or needs the wider $n{=}16$
    interpolation point.
    \item Decide on merging \code{feature/scale-controlled-redo} into \code{master} per the
    project's git workflow (merge, run test suite, delete branch) once the above is settled.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Currently in progress: Phase 1 item 2 --- noise-multiplier re-sweep}
The 8-client $\text{noise\_multiplier}=0.05$ sweet spot used throughout this report was never
re-checked at other client counts --- it could be a genuine property of the mechanism, or the sweet
spot itself could simply move as $n$ grows, independent of the scaling question this report
answers. A 96-combination sweep ($n \in \{8,16,32,48\} \times$ partition $\times$ privacy $\times$
six noise multipliers, \code{fedavg} only, fixed 20 rounds) is running now on the CUDA workstation
to test this directly. One concrete finding already surfaced: \code{global-dp} crashes with a
\code{ZeroDivisionError} at high noise multipliers ($\geq 0.25$) whenever a client's update collapses
to exactly zero norm --- \code{metricdp\_pytorch/metricdp\_strategy.py} already has a graceful
fallback for this exact situation, but \code{globaldp\_strategy.py} was never given the equivalent
fix. The sweep tolerates this (logs the failure, continues), and the fix will be applied and the
affected combinations rerun once the full sweep completes.

\subsection{Remaining Phase 1 work}
Two more items from \code{docs/RESEARCH\_ROADMAP.md}'s Phase 1 remain before this diagnosis phase
is closed out:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
    \item \textbf{Failure-mode/NaN logging in \code{runner.py}} before high-noise regimes ---
    motivated directly by the \code{ZeroDivisionError} failures above; not yet implemented.
    \item \emph{(Already done, for reference)}: instrumenting the raw pairwise-distance statistic
    is already on \code{master} --- \code{metricdp\_strategy.py} logs the full pairwise-distance
    distribution (median/mean/count), not just the single max used for calibration.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Phase 2: mechanism redesign}
Once Phase 1 is fully closed out, the roadmap's core novel contribution begins: refactoring
\code{MetricPrivacyServerSideFixedClipping} to accept a swappable distance function, and ablating
candidates (normalize-by-$n$, robust/median statistic, EMA-smoothed distance, subsampled pairwise
distance) against this report's constant-compute control across multiple $n$. A mechanism is
declared ``working'' only if it recovers/preserves the metric-privacy advantage across client
counts, not just at one $n$.
