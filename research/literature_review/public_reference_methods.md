# Public-reference and task-aware private releases: focused methods follow-up

2026-10-08. This is a bounded primary-source follow-up, not a rerun of the systematic review. The frozen 31-family counts remain unchanged. Included methods were read from the linked primary PDFs; the access log distinguishes inspected methods from deferred candidates. No untracked user PDF was read.

## Why this review now

The [matched CIA findings](../proposals/2026-10-07_matched_cia_findings.md) reject the tested radial/voting constructor as a near-chance improvement: at target AUC0.55, radial vote CE1.430741 loses to Gaussian non-vote CE1.354335 and uniform CE1.386294. At0.80, the same-query Gaussian matches radial. Those results motivate changing what is released before changing the noise density. They do not establish that compression or reference subtraction succeeds.

Three different operations must remain separate:

- **Projection:** retain coordinates in a public subspace. This reduces noise dimension but discards information.
- **Public-reference residual:** describe a client's change relative to a public predictor. This may concentrate useful signal, but subtracting a common reference alone leaves replacement differences unchanged.
- **Private distillation:** release bounded teacher answers on public inputs, then train only from protected answers. Its privacy comes from the release mechanism, not from the student model.

## 1. Gradient Embedding Perturbation (GEP)

Yu, Zhang, Chen and Liu, ICLR2021. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.12677). Read §2–3.3, Algorithm1–2, Theorems3.1–3.3 and AppendixD proof of3.1–3.2; printed pp4–8,16–17. Experimental implementation paragraph §4 inspected; empirical tables and full utility proof not audited.

**Source-derived explanation.** GEP computes anchor gradients on non-sensitive auxiliary examples, then uses power iterations to form an orthonormal row basis B. Labels may be random; the implementation assumes similar features rather than requiring correctly labelled public task data. Each private example gradient g becomes an embedding Bg and residual g−BᵀBg. These are separately clipped, summed and independently Gaussian-noised; reconstruction combines both parts. Small embedding dimension lowers one noise cost, while a small residual clipping radius lowers the other. Both releases consume privacy. AppendixD uses add/remove-one-record adjacency: sensitivities S1,S2 and standard deviations S1σ,S2σ yield order-α RDP α/σ² per step, composed across steps. The residual analysis assumes public/private gradients independently follow a common distribution; this is stronger than the practical auxiliary-feature assumption. Convex smooth utility bounds retain a residual-dependent ambient-dimension term. Remark1 explicitly qualifies unbiasedness: reconstruction is unbiased without clipping, whereas clipping generally introduces bias.

**Plain example / transfer deduction.** In a 51-coordinate head, five publicly chosen directions could capture the dominant task signal. GEP would release five coefficients plus a separately protected 51-coordinate residual; a projection-only control releases just the five coefficients. Measure discarded signal versus the residual's added noise rather than presuming the residual helps. This is the closest inspected precedent for the split, not evidence for a new noise law.

**Whole-client certificate required.** Replace per-example rows by one complete weighted client contribution and enforce public bounds on BOTH projected and residual vectors, with OUT contributing zero in the same slot. Per-record minibatch amplification does not transfer through sampling records inside an included client. Public anchors may depend on already protected history, but raw private PCA or private clipping radii need accounting. Never silently choose S2 from an observed residual maximum. Adding the residual also increases the observable channel; CIA must score its joint law, including unequal variances.

## 2. Projected DP-SGD (PDP-SGD)

Zhou, Wu and Banerjee, ICLR2021. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.03813). Read §2–3.2, Algorithm1, Theorems1–5 and intervening explanations; printed pp4–9. Theorem1's accompanying privacy argument read; generic-chaining and convergence appendix proofs not independently reconstructed.

**Source-derived explanation.** At a current model w, compute public gradients and their uncentered second-moment matrix M=Σg_pub g_pubᵀ/m. Its top-k eigenvectors define an orthogonal projector P. Compute a private minibatch gradient g, add isotropic Gaussian noise z, then update using P(g+z). The residual is discarded. Privacy is inherited from DP-SGD by conditional postprocessing: the public-data projector uses the current protected model, not a new unprotected private statistic. The stated privacy unit is one record. The utility analysis assumes public examples come from the private population, bounded gradients, gradient regularity and a useful eigengap. Reusing public examples is treated by a uniform concentration argument rather than pretending every iteration has independent fresh public data. The non-convex result first controls only the projected gradient; controlling the full gradient additionally requires the principal component to dominate its residual. The convex result assumes rank-k population gradient structure. Ordinary supervised public-gradient construction uses task labels.

**Plain example / transfer deduction.** If public gradients lie mostly along three head directions, noise outside those directions can be removed. If an important private class direction is absent publicly, the method also removes that learning signal. A small public eigengap means the selected basis can be unstable even though the privacy proof remains valid.

**Whole-client comparison.** Use the same public basis, complete client clip, decoder and conditional AUC calibration for projection-only, GEP-style split and full smooth-query controls. A public basis changes the task/sensitivity representation; it does not create record-to-client privacy automatically. Task-labelled public gradients exceed the current unlabelled-support information contract; randomized-label GEP and labelled PDP variants should be separate arms with separate public-only baselines.

## 3. Public pretraining followed by private fine-tuning

Li, Tramèr, Liang and Hashimoto, ICLR2022, *Large Language Models Can Be Strong Differentially Private Learners*. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.05679). Read §2, §3 effective-noise discussion/Eq1, §4.1–4.2 ghost-clipping construction and §7 limitations; printed pp2–3,5–7,11. No reconstruction of all accountant appendix details or empirical tables.

**Source-derived explanation.** Start from a public pretrained model and fine-tune using DP-Adam. Each training record's gradient is norm-clipped; Gaussian noise is added to the summed gradients and the iterative accesses are accounted. This is central record-level add/remove privacy, with the record defined by the task. Pretraining supplies useful representation and initialization; it does not protect the private fine-tuning by itself. The paper studies objective alignment and optimization choices rather than constructing a client-specific noise distribution. Its ghost-clipping method computes per-example gradient norms from layer activations and backward signals, then backpropagates a weighted loss to sum clipped gradients without materializing complete per-example vectors. This reduces memory requirements without changing the intended clipped-gradient mechanism. Larger models can perform better despite more noise coordinates. The authors explicitly caution that scraped pretraining data can itself be sensitive, and that private hyperparameter selection requires accounting. There is no universal theorem that public initialization makes arbitrary private residuals small or sufficient.

**Plain example / transfer deduction.** A strong public classifier already separates the four Fashion classes; a client then need only provide a correction. The private information can be useful only if it improves on that classifier under the protected observer contract. Giving this arm labelled public data while comparing it to unlabelled prototypes would credit the reference's information to privacy construction.

**Whole-client comparison.** A one-time clipped client parameter displacement or prediction correction is a different mechanism from the paper's record-clipped optimization. Certify its full client sensitivity directly. Keep a public-only learner and a central release of the identical correction. Fine-tuning all parameters, only a public low-dimensional head, or only a fixed public subspace are distinct utility controls; using public pretraining does not establish a dimensionality reduction theorem.

## 4. PATE: protected teacher answers and student postprocessing

Papernot, Song, Mironov, Raghunathan, Talwar and Erlingsson, ICLR2018, *Scalable Private Learning with PATE*. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.08908). Read §3.1–3.5, §4.1–4.3, Algorithms1–2 and AppendixA Proposition8 proof; printed pp3–8,17. Theorem6 statement and Proposition7 inspected; full data-dependent theorem and smooth-sensitivity proofs not audited here.

**Source-derived explanation.** Train independent teachers on disjoint private shards, then query them on public unlabelled inputs. GNMax adds independent Gaussian noise of variance σ² to each class count and releases the largest noisy count's class. One record can affect only one teacher vote, so the vote histogram changes by at most √2 in L2; the worst-case order-α RDP is α/σ². Queries compose. The student sees public inputs and protected labels, allowing arbitrary subsequent training as postprocessing. Confident-GNMax first privately tests consensus; its gate has its own cost even for unanswered queries. Interactive-GNMax compares teacher counts with current student predictions to avoid buying already-known answers. Strong consensus can support tighter data-dependent accounting, but publishing private cost estimates also needs sanitization. Teachers/raw votes remain outside the adversary's view. Student distillation does not protect teachers that are separately disclosed.

**Plain example / transfer deduction.** If all eight teachers agree that a prototype is class2, a protected answer may be useful; if counts are tied [2,2,2,2], the answer is noisy and costly. A public student reference can help choose questions whose answer changes learning, but raw consensus-based selection is not free.

**Whole-client boundary.** One teacher per fixed client, trained independently from that client's data and public context, lets one whole client change at most one vote per query. A fixed dummy vote also satisfies the √2 replacement bound; a zero-vote dummy uses a different sensitivity calculation. This is a derived transfer, not the paper's claimed privacy unit. Joint release across prototypes or adaptive query composition must still be calibrated. Eight teachers provide little redundancy compared with a large ensemble. A peer subtracting its own votes/noise needs the correct remaining-noise certificate. Per-client nonlinear reusable distributions cannot inherit an aggregate-only noise-share proof.

## What this implies for a bounded constructor comparison

These methods support a **representation-first comparison**, not a density-superiority claim. Freeze a basis/reference using explicitly public data and seeds. Start on development-only data; do not consume the remaining reserve to discover the rank, residual radius or teacher questions.

1. Separate information contracts: unlabelled/random-labelled anchors; genuinely task-labelled public reference; previously protected reference. Each gets its corresponding public-only control.
2. Compare a complete smooth descriptor, public projection only, projection plus separately clipped residual, and a public-reference correction using the same teacher/training inputs. Record useful signal outside the projection and clipping distortion. Public-reference correction r_i(D)=f_i(D)−f_public must explicitly become zero for OUT; subtracting f_public from an OUT-zero full teacher query instead would create a false nonzero dummy.
3. Use public joint sensitivity bounds and the actual observer law. For fixed reference f_public, r(D)−r(D')=f(D)−f(D'); reference subtraction alone cannot shrink replacement sensitivity. A smaller predeclared residual clip can reduce the protected dummy shift but may discard necessary signal. A data-dependent smaller maximum is research calibration, not a certified constructor.
4. Keep eight registered slots, fixed public weights and traffic/noise in OUT. Distinguish aggregate-only releases from individually reusable objects. Full-client clipping precedes release; label-balanced training and raw local priors may be internal computations but cannot reappear after protected release.
5. Compare at matched conditional CIA strength with strongest smooth-query controls, identical-query Gaussian and central controls. Evaluate equal IN/OUT utility and strongest known-alternative likelihood scores. A projected rank reduces coordinates, not automatically AUC: the target shift may still lie entirely in the retained space.

**Decision boundary:** the inspected literature establishes existing mechanisms for public subspace identification, residual perturbation and protected distillation. It does not show that a one-time client-specific distribution improves this project's near-chance CIA/utility tradeoff. A bounded experiment is justified only after an explicit public-information contract and uniform whole-client sensitivity certificate are fixed. No novelty or empirical success is asserted.

## Additional focused check: SCAFFOLD and a public class-conditional control variate

2026-10-08 follow-up after the negative public-reference residual comparison. This check motivates a development-only signal diagnostic; it is not a new private experiment or a SCAFFOLD reproduction.

Karimireddy, Kale, Mohri, Reddi, Stich and Suresh, ICML2020. [Official PMLR paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/karimireddy20a/karimireddy20a.pdf). Read §2 assumptions, §4 construction/Eqs3–6, Algorithm1, §5 TheoremIII and its proof sketch, and §6 TheoremIV statement and discussion; PDF pp2–6. Appendix convergence proofs and empirical results not audited.

**Source-derived tutorial.** SCAFFOLD maintains a client control vector c_i and a server vector c equal to the mean of the client controls. A local step uses g_i(y)−c_i+c, aiming to replace a drifting local direction with an estimate of the global direction. Client controls persist between rounds. One update option evaluates the client gradient at the server model; the cheaper option reconstructs a control from the local parameter displacement and step size. Clients communicate model and control changes; the server updates both states. Controls approximate client gradients, rather than class-conditional public means. TheoremIII gives optimization convergence bounds under smoothness and the stated stochastic-gradient assumptions, without requiring small client heterogeneity. Its proof sketch makes the limitation explicit: the corrected direction's discrepancy depends on how well c_i approximates the current client gradient. TheoremIV treats quadratic objectives with bounded Hessian dissimilarity. Neither result is a differential-privacy theorem, nor a guarantee that subtracting any public statistic decreases a client's release norm. [Method and convergence statements](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/karimireddy20a/karimireddy20a.pdf).

**Proposed diagnostic: our derivation, not the paper's mechanism.** Fix a genuinely public model B and labelled public class-gradient means γ_k=E_public[g(B,x,y) | y=k]. For client empirical class fractions p_ik, let c_i=Σ_k p_ik γ_k and r_i=g_i−c_i, where g_i is the full empirical mean gradient at B. All quantities must use the same model, loss, coordinates and class meanings. Then

`r_i = Σ_k p_ik (ḡ_ik − γ_k)`.

This removes the component predictable from a client's class mixture under the public conditional-gradient reference. It is a one-time public class-conditional control variate, not SCAFFOLD's persistent estimate of actual client gradients. The needed public class labels are additional task information. Local class fractions can remain internal, but any observable function of the resulting residual still needs protection.

For fixed public weights a_i with Σ_i a_i=1, define p̄_k=Σ_i a_i p_ik and g_public=Σ_k π_public,k γ_k. Algebra gives

`g_public + Σ_i a_i r_i = Σ_i a_i g_i + Σ_k (π_public,k − p̄_k) γ_k`.

Thus exact aggregate restoration holds before clipping/noise when the weighted pooled empirical class prior equals the public prior. This equality itself does not require matching public/private feature distributions. Under label shift (matching class-conditional distributions), the residuals may be small in expectation; finite samples and conditional distribution shift can defeat that expectation. Clipping generally destroys the exact equality.

**Why the diagnostic can fail.** Let c_i be the subtracted reference. Its norm effect is `||g_i−c_i||²−||g_i||² = ||c_i||²−2⟨g_i,c_i⟩`. Reduction needs sufficient positive alignment; a public conditional mean pointing against the actual client gradient increases the norm. Even norm reduction can remove useful task signal. Assess conditional-gradient agreement, alignment, clipping distortion and task curvature or loss change, not merely a favourable residual norm. SCAFFOLD's control-approximation argument supports this caution, not an automatic success claim.

**OUT and estimand boundary.** In the eight fixed-slot contribution game, OUT must explicitly upload zero residual while its traffic/noise remain. The public gradient stays fixed. With the same fixed weights, removing a client generally changes the pooled class prior, so the restoration equality need not hold in OUT even if it held in IN. In that case the released IN/OUT shift is a_i r_i, whereas the raw-gradient shift is a_i g_i: the construction intentionally changes the contribution being learned. Do not describe improved distinguishability as equal-utility protection of the original raw aggregate without measuring both worlds. A class-balanced public-reference objective can be legitimate, but it must be declared and compared against the strong public-only learner and identical-query central control.

**Certificate and next diagnostic boundary.** A predeclared public residual clip C makes the weighted residual contribution bounded for arbitrary local data; observed small norms or matched conditional means are not sensitivity certificates. Internal raw p_i does not need a separate release if it never leaves the query computation, but raw-dependent radius, public calibration or descriptor publication are not free. At fixed B, assess raw versus class-conditional residual signal on development data only, include mismatched public conditionals and pooled-prior discrepancy, and keep dummy contributions explicit. This check claims no distribution-constructor novelty, no SCAFFOLD privacy guarantee and no empirical superiority.
