Follow-up searched and methods-read 2026-10-01. This extends the mechanism-open direction; it does not change the frozen version-1 screening counts. Source-derived cards are deliberately bounded; the subsequent examples and CIA transfers are our deductions, not reported FL results.

## NGF1 — Staircase: optimize a scalar noise law

**Source-derived card.** Quan Geng and Pramod Viswanath, *The Optimal Mechanism in Differential Privacy*, [arXiv:1212.1186v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.1186), submitted 2012, revised 2013. The mechanism releases a scalar query plus noise independent of its output, with public sensitivity bound $\Delta$ and pure $\epsilon$-DP. Write $b=e^{-\epsilon}$. On the positive half-line its density is $a b^k$ on $[k\Delta,(k+\gamma)\Delta)$ and $a b^{k+1}$ on $[(k+\gamma)\Delta,(k+1)\Delta)$; reflect it about zero, with $a=(1-b)/[2\Delta(\gamma+b(1-\gamma))]$. Every $\gamma\in[0,1]$ is feasible. Optimize $\gamma$ for symmetric, nondecreasing error costs satisfying their growth condition. For absolute error, $\gamma^*=1/(1+e^{\epsilon/2})$. Algorithm 1 samples an equiprobable sign, geometric interval index, weighted Bernoulli subinterval and uniform position. Locators: §III, Properties 1–2 and Eqs. 32–40, pp. 8–9; Algorithm 1/Eq. 41, p. 9; §IV/Theorem 4, p. 9. The scalar optimality theorem concerns this output-independent class; the preceding reduction from output-dependent laws has an additional periodic/piecewise-constant technical condition (Theorem 2, pp. 6–7). It does not establish arbitrary vector or FL optimality.

**Own worked explanation.** With $\Delta=1$, $\epsilon=\log 2$, and $\gamma=1/2$, $b=1/2$ and $a=1/3$. Density on positive intervals $[0,.5),[.5,1),[1,1.5),[1.5,2)$ is respectively $1/3,1/6,1/6,1/12$, with a mirror image on the negative side. Thus the law has plateaus and descending steps, rather than a Gaussian bell. Its sampler chooses $G=k$ with probability $(1-b)b^k$ and the first subinterval with probability $\gamma/[\gamma+b(1-\gamma)]$; this is $2/3$ in the example. A uniform draw then chooses a point inside the selected half-interval. The example illustrates feasibility, not the optimal absolute-error value of $\gamma$.

**Own CIA transfer and construction cautions.** The scalar law could protect a publicly specified scalar client statistic or a low-dimensional projection. It becomes relevant only after specifying which neighboring client inputs can change that statistic and bounding the change. Applying independent staircases to $d$ coordinates at $\epsilon$ each generally gives a $d\epsilon$ composition bound, not an $\epsilon$ vector guarantee. A private client-specific $\gamma$, sensitivity or projection is an additional data-dependent mechanism; scalar feasibility for each fixed parameter does not prove privacy of their mixture. If the intended objective is record-level DP, construction must account for the distribution estimator as well as the transmitted noise. If the intended target is client identity under fixed participation, the CIA game still needs an explicit connection to that adjacency. These are proof obligations, not limitations solved by changing the density's name.

## NGF2 — K-norm: design the shape from permitted neighboring changes

**Source-derived card.** Moritz Hardt and Kunal Talwar, *On the Geometry of Differential Privacy*, [arXiv:0907.3754v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.3754), 2009 preprint. Initially databases are $x\in\mathbb R^n$, proximity is $\ell_1$ distance, and the query is a fixed linear map $F$. Let $K=F B_1^n$ and $\|z\|_K=\inf\{t:z\in tK\}$. The release density is $p_x(y)=\epsilon^d\exp[-\epsilon\|y-Fx\|_K]/[\Gamma(d+1)\operatorname{Vol}(K)]$. Sampling draws $r\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(d+1,\text{scale}=1/\epsilon)$ and $z$ uniformly from $K$, then returns $Fx+rz$. Theorem 4.3 proves pure $\epsilon$-DP by the triangle inequality: neighboring query shifts lie in $K$. Locators: §4, Definition 4.1/Eq. 5, Remark 4.2/Eq. 6, pp. 11–12. §9, pp. 21–22, extends the construction to general distance-normalized query differences and allows a containing convex body. Uniform convex-body sampling can be costly (§8); the simple mechanism can have poor error on some non-isotropic bodies (§7). Its broad near-optimality results require additional geometric assumptions; they are not universal FL performance guarantees.

**Own worked explanation.** Suppose every permitted neighboring output difference lies in the public rectangle $K=[-2,2]\times[-1,1]$. Its gauge is $\|z\|_K=\max(|z_1|/2,|z_2|)$. Consequently the density is proportional to $\exp[-\epsilon\max(|z_1|/2,|z_2|)]$: equal-density contours are rectangles. Draw two independent uniform coordinates from the rectangle, draw $r\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(3,1/\epsilon)$, and multiply both coordinates by that same radius. The noise components are therefore generally dependent, although the uniform-body coordinates in this particular sampler are independent. The public shape permits twice the scale along the first direction. It follows from the permitted changes, not an estimated covariance of observed client updates.

**Own CIA transfer and construction cautions.** A nonlinear client update can use the density proof if a fixed symmetric, bounded, full-dimensional convex body contains every relevant neighboring update difference. That transfers feasibility, not the original linear-query accuracy theorem. For example, replacement of one arbitrarily clipped client dataset can give a difference body twice the clipped-update body; changing one record during local training needs a separate bound. A lower-dimensional body requires working in its fixed public span; otherwise the displayed Lebesgue density is undefined. If each client builds $K_i$ from its current private gradients, the fixed-body proof no longer applies unchanged: changing the data changes the gauge and normalizer as well as the mean. Protected history, public structural constraints, or a proof controlling joint changes of the law are possible routes. Sec. 9 mentions adapting smooth-sensitivity ideas; it does not furnish a ready-made privacy theorem for an arbitrary learned body. This is particularly close to the project's distribution-construction question.

## NGF3 — Joseph–Yu: some tailored bodies have implementable samplers

**Source-derived card.** Matthew Joseph and Alexander Yu, *Some Constructions of Private, Efficient, and Optimal K-Norm and Elliptic Gaussian Noise*, [COLT 2024, PMLR 247:2723–2766](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v247/joseph24a.html). Their pure-DP part uses add/remove-record adjacency and the convex hull of the statistic's sensitivity space as the norm ball. For sums of vectors with at most $k$ nonzero entries, each bounded by one in absolute value, $K=kB_1^d\cap B_\infty^d$; its gauge is $\max(\|z\|_1/k,\|z\|_\infty)$. Lemmas 7–8 give the same exponential-gauge density and Gamma/uniform-body sampler. Their Sum sampler partitions the positive body into slices, weights slices by Eulerian numbers, samples a corresponding permutation/simplex, applies a volume-preserving map, and randomizes coordinate signs. Theorem 53 gives $O(d^2)$ arithmetic operations; the paper also constructs Count/Vote samplers. Locators: §2.1, p. 4; Problem 1, p. 2; §3.1/Lemma 17, pp. 6–8; Appendix B.1, Lemmas 46–53/Algorithm 1, pp. 17–20. “Optimal” refers to specified K-norm comparison orders, not every privacy mechanism or arbitrary learned FL distribution. The elliptic-Gaussian results are a separate concentrated-DP branch.

**Own worked explanation.** For $d=3,k=2$, the body is the cube $[-1,1]^3$ with corners cut away wherever $|z_1|+|z_2|+|z_3|>2$. The point $(1,1,1)$ is disallowed even though it lies in the coordinatewise bound; $(1,1,0)$ is allowed. This preserves a structural contribution constraint that a containing box loses. In this example the release law is proportional to $\exp[-\epsilon\max((|z_1|+|z_2|+|z_3|)/2,\max_j|z_j|)]$. After uniformly sampling this truncated body, multiply the point by $\operatorname{Gamma}(4,1/\epsilon)$. Body design and the radial tail law are separate choices.

**Own implementation note.** Appendix B's Lemma 18 prose appears to have an off-by-one count when describing generic simplex spacings. For a $d$-simplex with $d+1$ vertices, $d$ uniform cutpoints plus endpoints yield $d+1$ spacings. The fundamental simplex $0<x_1<\cdots<x_d<1$ can directly be sampled by sorting $d$ independent uniforms. Verify pseudocode indexing rather than copying that prose literally. This observation is not a challenge to the sensitivity-body argument.

**Own CIA transfer and construction cautions.** Sparse clipped/compressed client uploads could support this body only if contribution bounds hold for every input in the declared adjacency, including changes induced by private top-$k$ selection. If two uploads can change sparsity support under replacement adjacency, bounding each upload by this body gives differences in $2K$. A record-level local-training update need not satisfy the Sum statistic's record sensitivity simply because the eventual vector is sparse. Dense unrestricted gradients also do not inherit the paper's specialized sparse-statistic efficiency/performance claims. In addition, a sparsity mask sent separately is part of the observable transcript and needs coverage. The useful precedent is a complete chain from a contribution constraint to a body, a density and a sampler; it is not an already validated CIA defense.

## What this changes for the project — own synthesis

The candidate mechanism should remain $\widetilde u_i=u_i+Z_i$ with an explicitly constructed law $P_i$, rather than assuming $Z_i$ must be Gaussian. These foundations expose at least two design variables: scalar density shape (staircase) and vector geometry (K-norm). Neither requires empirically fitting a distribution to raw gradients. A client-specific estimator is one possible construction route; a body derived from public clipping/contribution constraints is another.

For a genuinely new proposed law, write its normalized density or probability mass function and sampler before evaluating it. Then test the actual law changes under the chosen neighboring inputs, including its private construction stage and metadata. A worst-case DP theorem, a population/average-case guarantee and empirical CIA AUC are distinct deliverables. If only empirical CIA mitigation is claimed, name that target and compare adaptive attacks and repeated-release transcripts. The existing frontier can define matched-utility empirical comparisons; it does not decide the optimal law or establish novelty. Prior art already contains non-Gaussian optimized additive laws and sensitivity-shaped multivariate noise, so “we used a unique distribution” is insufficient as the research contribution.
