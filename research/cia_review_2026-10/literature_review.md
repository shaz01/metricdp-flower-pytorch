# Protecting whole-client participation in federated learning: a literature review

Date: 2026-10-10. Prepared for the metric-privacy CIA project (`research/cia_review_2026-10/`). Order of work: the two local papers first, then a targeted search on client-level inference and metric/distributional privacy, then a broad sweep of FL privacy defenses. The corpus has 95 papers, each with a resolved DOI, a read-scope label and tags in [`paper_table.csv`](paper_table.csv); BibTeX keys in [`references.bib`](references.bib) match the table. Statements of the form "no work found" refer to this corpus and search, not to the whole literature.

## Bottom line

Client inference, deciding whether a whole client's dataset took part in training, is the least defended of the inference problems in federated learning (FL). Attacks exist in several forms (client participation [Sáinz-Pardo Díaz et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.knosys.2026.115993), user inference [Kandpal et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2024.emnlp-main.1014), subject membership [Suri et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2206.03317)), and they succeed most on clients whose data are atypical. Against this threat the literature offers worst-case client-level differential privacy (DP), which protects every client equally and pays for it in utility ([McMahan et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.1710.06963); [Geyer et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.1712.07557)), and the baseline's metric-inspired calibration, which rescales DP noise by the largest pairwise client-model distance without a guarantee [Sáinz-Pardo Díaz et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.knosys.2026.115993). The project's own matched-risk study found that this rescaling buys no utility once leakage is matched.

The review points away from further work on the noise distribution for three reasons. First, a formal toolbox for data-dependent noise exists (smooth sensitivity, propose-test-release, privately estimated clipping) and the baseline uses none of it, so its noise level is an unaccounted release ([Nissim et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1145/1250790.1250803); [Wang et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2209.07716); [Andrew et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.1905.03871)); formal user-level DP theory even scales error with the clients' concentration radius, the opposite of the implemented inverse-distance rule [Levy et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2102.11845). Second, distributional notions that credit the "crowd" with protection (Pufferfish, Bayesian DP, membership-inference privacy, GMIP) are defined at record level or as accountants, and our arithmetic on the GMIP bound shows that crowd randomness gives essentially nothing for full-model cross-silo FL with tens of clients ([Kifer and Machanavajjhala 2014](https://doi.org/10.1145/2514689); [Triastcyn and Faltings 2019](https://doi.org/10.1109/bigdata47090.2019.9005465); [Leemann et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2306.07273)). Third, defenses that shrink the client's distinguishing signal rather than masking it (generalization, distillation, consensus regularization, robust aggregation) have been developed for record-level membership inference and for source inference, but not for client participation ([Liu et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2110.05524); [Zhao et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.56553/popets-2026-0136)). Together these define the opportunity: a defense that suppresses the participation gap of atypical clients, evaluated with a defense-aware attack that also exploits metric privacy's noise-level side channel.

## 1. The client-inference threat

### Four client-granularity questions

Federated learning admits four different questions at client granularity, and the literature often conflates them. *Participation* asks whether a client's dataset was used at all; this is the baseline's client inference attack (CIA), in which a semi-honest participant holding a shadow sample of the target compares the global model's loss on that sample across rounds [Sáinz-Pardo Díaz et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.knosys.2026.115993). *Membership* asks whether a record was used, and in FL the strongest attacks combine many clients' updates over rounds ([Nasr et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp.2019.00065); [Zhu et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1109/cvpr52734.2025.01922); survey in [Bai et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1145/3704633)). *Source attribution* asks which client holds a known training record ([Hu et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1109/icdm51629.2021.00129); [Hu et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1109/tdsc.2023.3321565)). *Property inference* asks what a client's data look like ([Melis et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp.2019.00029); [Hartmann et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1109/satml54575.2023.00018); [Pejó and Biczók 2023](https://doi.org/10.1109/tbdata.2023.3280406)). Game-based systematization makes these relations explicit and shows that guarantees for one do not transfer automatically to another [Salem et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp46215.2023.10179281).

Participation inference has close analogues outside the baseline. User inference on fine-tuned language models needs only black-box access and a few samples from the user that need not be the training samples, and it reaches near-perfect success in some settings [Kandpal et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2024.emnlp-main.1014). Subject membership inference in cross-silo FL assumes the adversary knows the subject's distribution rather than its records and the attacks are reported to be extremely potent [Suri et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2206.03317). Both match the CIA threat more closely than record-level MIA does, because the attacker's knowledge is distributional.

### Concentrated vulnerability

Every line of evidence in the corpus agrees that client-level leakage is heterogeneous. Users leak more when they are outliers, when their examples are correlated, and when they contribute a larger share of the data [Kandpal et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2024.emnlp-main.1014). Correlated records strengthen membership attacks and weaken DP's empirical protection, and a client's records are correlated by construction [Humphries et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1109/csf57540.2023.00013). Removing the most exposed points exposes the next layer [Carlini et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2206.10469), and per-example privacy loss under DP-SGD tracks gradient norms, leaving poorly fitted groups worse protected ([Yu et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2206.02617); [Feldman and Zrnic 2020](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2008.11193)). In FL specifically, clustering clients into small personalized groups increases membership risk for minorities [Jung et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2503.09414), and source-inference risk concentrates in outlier clients [Zhao et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.56553/popets-2026-0136). The project's EuroSAT frontier reproduces the pattern: per-target scores span 0.20-0.99 under the same mechanism and noise (`project_state.md`, Figure 1b).

The population quantity a participation attacker estimates is the *participation gap*, the performance difference between participating and non-participating client distributions [Yuan et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2110.14216). At record level, membership advantage is bounded through the generalization gap [Yeom et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1109/csf.2018.00027); the analogous client-level link, from participation gap to CIA advantage, has not been formalized in this corpus and is the basis of direction D1.

## 2. Metric privacy and data-dependent calibration

### Formal metric privacy

Metric privacy (d-privacy) generalizes DP to secrets in a metric space, so that indistinguishability degrades gracefully with the distance between secrets [Chatzikokolakis et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-39077-7_5); geo-indistinguishability is its planar-Laplace instance [Andrés et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.1212.1984). In machine learning, a von Mises-Fisher gradient mechanism gives both epsilon-DP and epsilon-d-privacy with angular distance and is reported to outperform Gaussian noise in the utility-privacy trade-off, with an empirical comparison against reconstruction and membership attacks [Faustini et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2211.04686); formal d-privacy has also been applied to personalized FL across heterogeneous client groups [Galli et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.5220/0011885000003405). In all of these, the metric acts on the secret and the mechanism's law is fixed in advance.

### The baseline's calibration

The baseline uses the metric as a data-dependent multiplier: the server sets the noise standard deviation to zC/(n d_t), with d_t the maximum mean-layer distance between client models in round t, and explicitly disclaims a formal guarantee [Sáinz-Pardo Díaz et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.knosys.2026.115993). Three consequences follow from the paper and the project record. The utility advantage reported against global DP is not measured at matched protection, and the project's matched-risk study found the two mechanisms equivalent once leakage was matched. The rule is anti-aligned with the vulnerability pattern of §1: because d_t is a maximum, an outlier client raises it and lowers the noise exactly when the most identifiable client participates (our deduction; in the 3-client CIA runs the target is in the maximum-distance pair in 97-98% of rounds). And the noise level is itself a function of who participates, so it can leak participation (`project_state.md`, Figure 3). The paper's own prose on page 6 describes the opposite direction ("greater divergence requires stronger perturbation"), which is also the direction formal user-level DP takes (§3).

### Formal data-dependent noise

Formal alternatives to a raw data-dependent noise scale have existed for two decades. Smooth sensitivity calibrates noise to a smooth upper bound of the local sensitivity with a full DP guarantee [Nissim et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1145/1250790.1250803); propose-test-release now has Rényi-DP accounting suitable for many adaptive robust queries such as robust SGD [Wang et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2209.07716); and adaptive clipping in FL spends a small budget to release a private quantile of update norms that then sets the clipping radius [Andrew et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.1905.03871). The risk these tools address is real: choosing hyperparameters from non-private runs leaks information [Papernot and Steinke 2021](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2110.03620), and system components outside the model create privacy side channels [Debenedetti et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2309.05610). The direct implication for the baseline is that any dispersion statistic used to set noise must be released privately and accounted for; a privately estimated distance quantile, by analogy with adaptive clipping, is the minimal fix.

## 3. Worst-case client-level DP

User-level DP for FL clips each client's update, bounds aggregation weights, samples clients and adds Gaussian noise to the sum ([McMahan et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.1710.06963); [Geyer et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.1712.07557)); convergence-privacy trade-offs for noisy aggregation were analysed by [Wei et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1109/tifs.2020.2988575). Theory for user-level DP shows that private mean estimation can achieve error scaling with the concentration radius of the users' distribution rather than with the full range, so that the price of privacy falls when users agree [Levy et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2102.11845). This is the formal counterpart of the intuition behind metric calibration, and it runs in the direction opposite to the implemented formula: concentration (small dispersion) permits less noise. Practical refinements include sharpness-aware local training that shrinks update norms and makes them more robust to noise ([Shi et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1109/tpami.2025.3597922)), sparsified perturbation [Hu et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1109/tmc.2023.3343288), time-adaptive privacy spending [Kiani et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2502.18706), DP-FTRL without sampling assumptions [Kairouz et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2103.00039), user-level sampling for LLM fine-tuning [Charles et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2407.07737), and amplification by subsampling ([Balle et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.1807.01647); for the MI precision bound, [Thudi et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2202.12232)). Individualized budgets exist at example level ([Boenisch et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2303.17046); [Feldman and Zrnic 2020](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2008.11193)).

Empirical audits show that these worst-case guarantees are loose in realistic FL threat models. Crafted canary clients give per-round empirical epsilon 4-5 times below the theoretical bound [Maddock et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2210.02912), and one-shot estimation with random canary clients measures privacy loss within the training run itself [Andrew et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2302.03098). Record-level auditing establishes the tools for lower bounds ([Jagielski et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2006.07709); [Nasr et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp40001.2021.00069); [Nasr et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2302.07956); [Steinke et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2305.08846)). The looseness is the space that distributional notions (§4) try to exploit and that a defense-aware CIA evaluation (§6) must measure.

## 4. Distributional privacy notions

Pufferfish is the general framework for privacy definitions that depend on a data-generating prior; DP is the special case with independent records and an arbitrary prior [Kifer and Machanavajjhala 2014](https://doi.org/10.1145/2514689). Bayesian DP restricts the differing record to the data distribution and gives sharper accounting; its FL version reports client-level epsilon below 1 for similarly distributed clients and correspondingly less noise ([Triastcyn and Faltings 2019](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.1901.09697); [Triastcyn and Faltings 2019](https://doi.org/10.1109/bigdata47090.2019.9005465)). Membership-inference privacy bounds the attacker's success directly and needs less randomness than DP ([Izzo et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2211.06582)), and GMIP strengthens this to a bound on the whole trade-off curve of a membership test, derived for noisy SGD, together with an analytical likelihood-ratio attack (GLiR) that needs no shadow models [Leemann et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2306.07273). PAC privacy calibrates noise by simulating the algorithm over the data distribution [Xiao and Devadas 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-38545-2_20); noiseless privacy obtains protection from data randomness alone [Farokhi 2021](https://doi.org/10.1109/tbdata.2021.3104021); and a relaxed threat model in which the adversary lacks the exact training database but holds related or partial data, which is precisely the CIA attacker's position, has been characterized in hypothesis-testing terms [Kaissis et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.52202/075280-2435). Distribution privacy protects global properties of a dataset rather than records [Chen and Ohrimenko 2022](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2207.08367). On the utility side, DP-GD can be free of excess risk in over-parameterized random-features models [Bombari and Mondelli 2024](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2410.14787), and decentralized learning can reduce attack efficacy without noise by fragmenting models across virtual nodes [Biswas et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.56553/popets-2025-0043).

None of these notions is defined for whole-client participation as a hypothesis test. Bayesian DP comes closest at client level, but it is an accountant whose guarantee depends on the assumed client distribution, and it was not evaluated against participation attacks.

### GMIP at client level

Lifting GMIP from records to clients replaces the per-example gradient with a client's clipped update, the batch size with the number of clients aggregated per round, and the background randomness with the between-client variability of updates. Applying the paper's one-step result (Corollary 5.1) with a typical client gives a noiseless parameter of about sqrt(2d/(1+2n)) for d shared parameters and n clients ([`scripts/gmip_regime_calculator.py`](scripts/gmip_regime_calculator.py)). For the project's EuroSAT network (d = 289,194, n = 48) this is about 77, so the crowd provides no meaningful protection, and when noise must be added the bound needs roughly 269 times the GDP noise standard deviation for mu = 1, so GDP is the operative guarantee. The bound helps only when n is comparable to d: the GMIP paper's last-layer CIFAR-10 setting (d = 650, batch 1000) and a scalar loss statistic (d = 1, n = 48) need no noise, while a 132-parameter head with 8 clients still needs 5.6 times the GDP noise ([`data/gmip_regimes.csv`](data/gmip_regimes.csv); [`notes/local_papers_notes.md`](notes/local_papers_notes.md)). This is our arithmetic on the printed formula, not a theorem for FL. It explains why "crowd" arguments cannot justify metric calibration for full-model cross-silo FL, and it locates where distributional guarantees can pay: low-dimensional shared objects, many clients per round, or attackers restricted to low-dimensional statistics.

## 5. Signal-reduction defenses

Generalization-based defenses reduce the signal a membership attacker measures. Adversarial regularization [Nasr et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1145/3243734.3243855), loss relaxation [Chen et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2207.05801), low-confidence training [Chen and Pattabiraman 2024](https://doi.org/10.14722/ndss.2024.23014) and self-distillation ensembles [Tang et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2110.08324) all target record-level MIA, and generalization techniques have been reported to outperform DP empirically against MIA [Liu et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2110.05524). The effect is not uniform: sharpness-aware minimization can increase membership risk despite better generalization [Kim et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2310.00488), so flatter training (as in DP-FedSAM) is not automatically a privacy gain.

FL-specific signal-reduction defenses are recent and target other questions. FinP combines risk-weighted server aggregation with client-side regularization against localized overfitting to equalize source-inference risk across clients [Zhao et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.56553/popets-2026-0136). CoFedMID limits local memorization and adds coalition-cancelling noise against trajectory-based membership attacks [Bai et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2601.06866). Client-level input perturbation shifts each client's data distribution and reports MIA near chance at no more than 0.7% accuracy loss [Yang et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1109/dsn58367.2023.00037). Robust aggregation mitigates malicious-client distribution inference [Xu et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1145/3589335.3651555), and causal learning resists distributional membership inference [Hartmann et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1109/satml54575.2023.00018). For user inference, deduplication and capping a user's contribution give partial mitigation [Kandpal et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2024.emnlp-main.1014). None of these has been evaluated against client participation inference.

Robust statistics and privacy are formally linked: robust estimators can be turned into private ones with near-optimal rates ([Hopkins et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1145/3564246.3585115); [Asi et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2302.01855)), and centered clipping around the aggregation history bounds each worker's influence relative to a consensus [Karimireddy et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2012.10333). A client whose update is pulled toward the crowd before aggregation is harder to detect, which motivates direction D4, but the project's earlier public-residual and centering experiments (`research/proposals/2026-10-08_*`) found little headroom in a one-shot head step, so this route needs a multi-round test before any claim.

Personalization changes what is shared. Keeping personalization layers or heads local ([Arivazhagan et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.1912.00818); [Collins et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2102.07078); [Liang et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2001.01523)) reduces the dimension and the client-specific content of the released model, which by §4 is where distributional protection becomes effective, but clustering-based personalization can concentrate risk in small groups [Jung et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2503.09414). Cryptographic and architectural defenses ([Bonawitz et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1145/3133956.3133982); [Kairouz et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2102.06387); [Agarwal et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2110.04995); parameter-level shuffling against source inference in [Athanasiou et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2603.02017)) hide individual updates from the server but leave the aggregate, and therefore the CIA signal available to a peer, unchanged. Reconstruction defenses (representation perturbation, bottlenecks, instance hiding) target a different attack and have known weaknesses ([Sun et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1109/cvpr46437.2021.00919); [Scheliga et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1109/wacv51458.2022.00366); [Huang et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2010.02772); [Carlini et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp40001.2021.00099); [Huang et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2112.00059)).

## 6. Credible CIA measurement

The membership-inference literature converged on likelihood-ratio tests and on reporting the true-positive rate at low false-positive rates, because average-case AUC hides the few individuals who are reliably identified ([Carlini et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp46214.2022.9833649); [Ye et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1145/3548606.3560675)). In FL, the strongest record attacks use the updates of all clients across rounds [Zhu et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1109/cvpr52734.2025.01922), and GLiR shows that an analytical likelihood-ratio test on aggregated gradients can replace shadow models when the background distribution can be estimated [Leemann et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2306.07273). The baseline's CIA uses a pooled AUC over 20 dependent rounds of one IN and one OUT trajectory [Sáinz-Pardo Díaz et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.knosys.2026.115993), and the project's frontier uses a folded paired concordance; both are diagnostics rather than trial-level ROC estimates (`research/project_evidence_audit.md`). A credible comparison against metric privacy therefore needs many independent targets and federations, a defense-aware attacker that may use the update sequence and the noise level, and TPR at fixed low FPR alongside AUC. Canary-client auditing ([Maddock et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2210.02912); [Andrew et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2302.03098)) provides a complementary, attack-independent measurement at client level.

## 8. Gaps tied to this project

The gaps below are anchored to what the corpus does and does not contain and to the project's own measurements; each maps to a direction in [`research_directions.md`](research_directions.md).

1. **No defense against client participation that reduces the participation signal.** All CIA defenses in the corpus add noise; signal-reduction defenses exist for record MIA and SIA ([Zhao et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.56553/popets-2026-0136); [Bai et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2601.06866)) but not for participation. (D1)
2. **The baseline's calibration is unaccounted and plausibly leaky,** while formal data-dependent tools exist ([Nissim et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1145/1250790.1250803); [Andrew et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.1905.03871)) and the formal user-level analogue points the other way [Levy et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2102.11845). No adaptive attack on metric-calibrated noise exists in the corpus. (D2)
3. **No hypothesis-testing guarantee for whole-client participation.** GMIP is record-level and Bayesian DP is an accountant; our regime analysis says a client-level version is only useful for low-dimensional shared objects or large rounds. (D3, D6)
4. **Heterogeneous vulnerability is documented but not addressed under a formal guarantee.** Outlier clients dominate leakage ([Kandpal et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2024.emnlp-main.1014); [Zhao et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.56553/popets-2026-0136)), yet worst-case DP is uniform and metric calibration lowers noise when outliers are present. (D4)
5. **The temporal profile of participation leakage is unexploited.** Time-adaptive spending targets utility [Kiani et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2502.18706); user inference concentrates early in LLM fine-tuning [Kandpal et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2024.emnlp-main.1014); the project's clean-view gap is largest in rounds 1-25. (D5)
6. **CIA evaluation practice lags MIA practice.** Neither the baseline nor any CIA paper reports TPR at low FPR over many independent targets with a defense-aware attacker ([Carlini et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp46214.2022.9833649)). (D2, prerequisite for all)

## 9. Open uncertainties

The corpus is broad but shallow outside the two local papers: most external entries were read at abstract level, so mechanism details and reported numbers should be checked in full text before they are used as comparators (priority: [Zhao et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.56553/popets-2026-0136), [Triastcyn and Faltings 2019](https://doi.org/10.1109/bigdata47090.2019.9005465), [Levy et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2102.11845), [Galli et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.5220/0011885000003405), [Kandpal et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2024.emnlp-main.1014), [Suri et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2206.03317)). The GMIP regime numbers rely on a CLT-based approximation whose finite-sample error is not certified in the main text, and the client-level lift is our proposal. The side-channel magnitudes in the project's logs are one-round counterfactual estimates, not attack results. Finally, citation indexing for 2025-2026 work is incomplete, so a quick forward search on the baseline paper and on GMIP should be repeated before any novelty claim is written.
