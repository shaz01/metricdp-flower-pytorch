# Independent protected-real-history mathematics review

2026-10-05. Scope: review of the proposed offline bias-contrast protected-probe calculation. This is a conditional mechanism and developmental quadratic-utility diagnostic, not a certified private training trajectory or CIA evaluation. No code/results changes or additional experiments are authorized by this review.

## Conditional privacy certificate

Fix public history, the three-dimensional contrast basis, profile map, clipping banks, aggregation weights and all tuning choices. Clip the early local query into the equal-axis weighted L1 body of radius C0 and add independent coordinate Laplace noise of scale C0/epsilon0. Relative to a fixed zero-contribution dummy, every clipped nonempty query has body gauge at most one, so its upload is epsilon0-DP on that edge. This is the contribution/dummy calibration: all-pairs replacement would require twice this parameter or twice the scale.

Given the already released probe transcript, selecting the main profile by the deterministic map of `argmax(B @ noisy_probe)` costs no new RR budget. The selected body is the same for both inputs when evaluating a conditional likelihood ratio at the same observed history. The main query, clipped in this body, receives coordinate Laplace noise of scale r/epsilon1. With zero dummy contribution its conditional shift has gauge at most one, giving epsilon1 on the dummy edge uniformly over every selected profile and history. Adaptive composition therefore certifies epsilon0+epsilon1=E for the hypothetical joint target-client transcript. Hiding either transcript or releasing fixed postprocessed aggregates does not increase that bound. This gives at most 2E between arbitrary nonempty inputs by chaining through dummy; it is not an E all-pairs replacement certificate.

The empty slot must still draw a probe, select a profile from that probe, and draw the main upload noise. Its query is zero in both stages. Its public weight and traffic cannot disclose emptiness. The main noise must be fresh and conditionally independent of the probe. In a complete interactive protocol, other observable clients/history must themselves be postprocessing of protected messages with no additional direct dependence on the target input.

Saved early/late updates and raw global checkpoints do not satisfy that full protocol by themselves. Treating checkpoints as externally fixed public anchors permits the conditional clipping lemma, but does not retroactively privatize the process that produced those checkpoints. Raw developmental derivatives, data-dependent weights and geometry/coefficient/budget calibration likewise remain unaccounted. A fixed public argmax profile constructor avoids *new* selector cost; the probe's privacy and utility cost remains in the total.

## Two-signal quadratic objective and correlation

For each probe draw let A be its weighted aggregate and B the weighted aggregate of main clipped queries selected using those same probes. Let Z be the fresh main aggregate noise. The final update is delta=a A+b(B+Z), with a,b in [0,1]. Conditional on all probes, E[Z]=0 and

    Cov(Z | probes) = 2/epsilon1² * sum_i alpha_i² diag(r_{k_i}²).

For fixed global developmental gradient g and positive-semidefinite Hessian H, expected second-order loss change is

    a gᵀ E[A] + b gᵀ E[B]
    + .5 a² E[Aᵀ H A] + a b E[Aᵀ H B]
    + .5 b² {E[Bᵀ H B] + E[tr(H Cov(Z | probes))]}.

The cross moment E[AᵀHB] is essential: B depends on the probe noise through profile selection. Replacing it by E[A]ᵀH E[B] discards selection correlation. The main-noise contraction can be integrated analytically for each probe draw because fresh Z has conditional zero mean, even when its covariance depends on the selected profile. There is no missing A/Z or B/Z cross term under that freshness condition.

The 2-by-2 Hessian in (a,b) has entries E[AHA], E[AHB], E[BHB]+E[tr(H Cov Z)]. It is positive semidefinite: its probe part is an average Gram matrix in the H seminorm and the noise term adds a nonnegative lower-right entry. Box minimization must examine a valid interior stationary point, edge optima and corners, handling singular matrices. No-op (a,b)=(0,0) ensures the optimum score cannot exceed zero. Coefficients multiply both each signal and its corresponding noise; applying shrink only to the main mean would be incorrect.

When evaluating fitted coefficients on fresh probes, keep the full per-draw expression above, including its cross product and conditional main-noise penalty. A paired standard error computed from per-probe differences estimates Monte Carlo uncertainty of the *integrated-main-noise developmental objective*. It does not measure variation over trained models, sampled clients, label partitions, actual realized main noise, or held-out classification loss. Fitting on 4096 probes and evaluating on independent 16384 probes avoids noise-draw evaluation tuning, provided no evaluated score chooses a new arm/configuration.

## Controls and interpretation

The matched public-static arm must use exactly the adaptive arm's probe budget/radius and coefficient domain while fixing the main profile independently of private probe realizations. The globally optimized static arm may optimize the same full grid. Both need the same dummy-edge Laplace calibration, main-body candidates and covariance calculation. The first signal need not be discarded merely because its profile selection is static: optimizing a and b gives this control access to both protected signals.

A one-release control using the public source query lambda*u_early+(1-lambda)*u_late, full budget E, all four profiles or the independent public-mixture bank, the same radius grid and shrink [0,2] is a useful stronger diagnostic. Fixed output scaling is postprocessing and can preserve an E dummy certificate while matching the possible two-signal coefficient sum up to two. It is a finite candidate family, not the optimizer over every source combination/noise law. Public random-mixture sampling must include its profile variance in expected loss, and its random profile must be independent of private input.

Client-slot permutation equivariance follows only if the same local rule is used in every slot and updates, weights and random innovations move together. The public four-class contrast map remains fixed: slot equivariance is distinct from invariance under arbitrary class relabeling. A source path from round0 to5 or5 to20 is an offline pair of anchored queries; the late query does not arise from the noisy aggregate of this probe. A favorable result would establish restricted utility headroom under this anchored construction, not a coupled FL convergence result or superiority to metric privacy. A failure would also be limited to this bank, profile rule and developmental quadratic proxy.

## Actual implementation check

Read `research/calculations/protected_real_history_probe.py` after it became available, without running it. No material mathematical implementation discrepancy was found. `probe` uses the full equal-axis L1 clipped early update and unit-scale independent Laplace innovations multiplied by C0/epsilon0. Its class scores reconstruct the four-dimensional zero-sum bias vector through the fixed Helmert basis; profile choice accesses the protected local upload, not raw class counts. `second` clips the entire late three-vector in its selected weighted L1 body. Its covariance contraction is exactly 2 sum alpha_i² sum_j r_ij² H_jj / epsilon1².

`fit` averages complete AHA, AHB and BHB terms, adding the conditional main-noise trace to the BB matrix entry. `values` applies both coefficients to their means, retains the cross product through the combined vector, and adds .5*b² times the main trace. The resulting fitted objective matches the formula above. `box_minimum` enumerates the interior, all box faces and corners, uses a least-squares stationary solution for singular faces and checks its residual before accepting it. This handles the convex two-variable objective without assuming an invertible Hessian; no-op is included among the corners.

The matched static family fixes the adaptive winner's probe radius and budget split, then independently retunes the main radius/profile and both coefficients. The optimized static family searches the same full probe/split grid as the adaptive family. The one-release arm uses all E through `moments(..., eta=2*E)`, which correctly converts the reused replacement-kernel parameter to contribution/dummy scales r/E. It includes all deterministic profile vertices through the 287 public-mixture bank, includes selector dispersion for random public profiles, and optimizes a [0,2] output shrink. Its expectation is integrated exactly for the developmental quadratic; it therefore needs no probe Monte Carlo evaluation or noise interval. The denominator cutoff at 1e-20 is a numerical convention, rather than an exact statement about arbitrarily tiny quadratic costs.

Fit and evaluation unit-Laplace arrays use distinct deterministic seeds, with 4096 fitting and 16384 evaluation draws. Configurations are chosen only from the fitting arrays; the fresh evaluation arrays enter `evaluate` and paired contrasts after configuration selection. The one-release optimum uses exact fixed-development moments, with no fresh-noise fit advantage needed. All arms in a cell share evaluation innovations for paired differences. Different budgets and partition cells also reuse innovations, so their reported Monte Carlo errors cannot be treated as independent replications or independent multiple-test evidence.

Only cached training Arrow examples and saved developmental indices are loaded; no new held-out examples are accessed. Saved late-round raw models determine the common developmental gradient/Hessian and saved raw early/late updates define the two queries. This follows the explicitly anchored replay scope, with the preceding accounting limitations intact. The self-check includes empirical first/main objective consistency, matched-innovation slot permutation and fixed-assignment main covariance checks; these support implementation consistency but do not constitute an end-to-end privacy proof.

## Saved-result verification and interpretation

Independently read `results/client_specific_noise/protected_real_history_probe.json` and `protected_real_history_trials.npz`; did not rerun the calculation. Verified 18 path/seed/partition rows, 54 budget cases and 162 saved arm arrays, each with 16384 probe draws. Recomputed all 162 paired contrast means, standard errors and normal intervals from the saved arrays, including the exact one-release reference score as a constant paired vector. Also recomputed each arm's logged evaluation mean and standard error. All values agree to absolute tolerance 1e-15.

On fresh protecting-noise draws, adaptive has a lower developmental quadratic mean than same-probe static in 18 cases, globally optimized static in 12, and the stronger one-release family in six. Every negative paired contrast has a negative upper endpoint in its reported pointwise 95% normal interval. The six one-release improvements are exactly label-stress E=8, both recorded paths and all three seeds. Their gains range from 0.0001093824446 to 0.0002467982303. No case meets the 0.001 material-gain flag. Gains against the weaker controls can exceed that threshold, but cannot substitute for the registered stronger one-release comparison.

The largest gain against one-release is seed44, label stress, recorded path5→20, E=8. Its fitted adaptive configuration uses probe/main budgets4/4, radii.3/.3 and coefficients[0.4634685499,1]. Fresh-probe adaptive mean change is −0.001691703106, globally optimized static −0.000911370433, same-probe static −0.000786848681, and exact one-release −0.001444904875. Thus the adaptive-minus-one-release difference is −0.0002467982303; these quantities are expected quadratic loss changes at the same developmental raw anchor, not directly evaluated held-out cross-entropy changes.

The directional result supports a narrowly stated observation: a protected noisy early query can sometimes select a useful geometry without paying a separate fresh RR cost, while retaining its own budget and utility cost. It does not show that the probe is free, that this classifier/profile bank is optimal, or that a private coupled FL trajectory attains the same benefit. Noise-fit/evaluation separation addresses Monte Carlo fitting optimism, not raw-data calibration or developmental-data reuse. Reported intervals quantify conditional fresh-probe Monte Carlo uncertainty with main noise analytically integrated; they supply neither held-out-data uncertainty nor multiple-comparison-adjusted research confirmation. With no 0.001 passes against the stronger reference, this is bounded design evidence rather than an implementation/large-experiment mandate.
